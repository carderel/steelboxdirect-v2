## zoning[] — THE HONEST HOUSTON STORY (do not "fix" this into a conventional zoning list)

**The oddity, stated plainly:** the City of Houston is the largest U.S. city with **no conventional zoning code** — voters rejected zoning in 1948, 1962, and 1993. Development inside the city is governed by ordinance codes (notably Chapter 42, the land development ordinance) administered by the Houston Planning & Development Department, plus private deed restrictions. Confirmed directly on the city's own Development Regulations page: *"The City of Houston does not have zoning, but development is governed by ordinance codes that address how property can be subdivided."* The city even publishes an annual official "No Zoning Letter" (e.g. houstontx.gov/planning//DevelopRegs/docs_pdfs/2026/No-Zoning-Letter-and-Boundary-Map-2026.pdf). **Texas counties likewise have no zoning power in unincorporated areas** — county entries below are development/floodplain **permitting** authorities, not zoning offices. Page copy should say "development & permitting resources," not "zoning offices," for this city.

## geography

**Honest sentences (1–2, delivery-relevant):**
Houston sits on the flat Gulf Coastal Plain, where clay-heavy soils drain slowly and much of the metro lies in or near mapped floodplains — the Harris County Flood Control District notes a major flood occurs somewhere in the county about every two years, so plan container placement on firm, elevated ground that stays solid after heavy rain. Access is strong via I-10, I-45, I-69, and the I-610 loop.
