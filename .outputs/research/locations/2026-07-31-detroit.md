## zoning[] (graded; below C omitted)

**Michigan reality (must be represented honestly):** under the Michigan Zoning Enabling Act (Act 110 of 2006, legislature.mi.gov), zoning in Michigan is adopted and administered at the **city/township/village level**, not by counties, in nearly all of Metro Detroit. The entries below are the honest county-level pointers: the city zoning department for Detroit proper, and county planning offices that maintain land-use/zoning resources and links to each local community's ordinance. Honest prose (for delivery/body copy — Great Lakes winters + freeze-thaw placement realities, verified):

> Metro Detroit sits on flat, former lake-plain ground along the Detroit River and Lake St. Clair — easy grades for delivery trucks, but Great Lakes winters mean spring freeze-thaw can leave unpaved sites soft; a gravel pad and a placement date outside the thaw window make delivery day smoother. Michigan also posts seasonal spring weight restrictions ("frost laws") on many county and local roads, which can affect truck routing in late winter and early spring.

- Evidence: Michigan spring weight restrictions verified via MDOT press releases (michigan.gov/mdot, Feb–May 2026 cycle) and Michigan County Road Association (county/local road agencies set their own restrictions). Grade A.
- Terrain: lake-plain flatness is standard physical geography of the region; kept qualitative.
