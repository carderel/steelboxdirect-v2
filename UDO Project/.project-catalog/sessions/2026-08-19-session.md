## The verifier measured rather than grepped, and two methods are worth reusing

**On the redefined city-page hard stop** it did not search for dollar signs. It extracted every `$N` token
from the **visible DOM** of all fifteen city pages, with `ld+json`, `script` and `style` stripped, and
**set-differenced them against that metro's own feed values.** Zero strays on any page. Every page carries
its centroid ZIP, its effective date and the disclaimer.

**On the empty-feed path** it did not read the fallback and trust it. It rsynced the tree to a scratch copy,
emptied the module, set `lastVerified = null`, rebuilt, and confirmed 56 pages with **zero `NaN`, `Invalid
Date`, `undefined` or `$,`** anywhere. City price blocks vanish entirely, the metro table is absent rather
than blank, national figures fall back to baseline.

## It declined to confirm one claim, and was right to

"Dayton is the only metro where the two price bases differ" is **inconclusive offline**, because
`baseDeliveryCost` is deliberately not serialised into the module, so `deliveryUnitPrice` is not recoverable
from it. It named what would resolve it, a dry-run harvest with network access, and noted the claim is not
load-bearing: the script never reads `fromPrice` into a figure regardless of how many metros diverge.

That is the right shape of answer. The claim is about live data, not about the code.

## The first commit attempt stalled, and the cause was the brief

Stalled at 600s with no progress and **no commits made**, verified: HEAD still `e3fc422`, index empty, 34
deletions unstaged, both landmines untracked. Cause: the brief handed it eight detailed commit bodies full of
reasoning, which implicitly invited it to re-confirm every claim in them, work the verifier had already

that the justification is established, or a careful agent will try to earn it.
