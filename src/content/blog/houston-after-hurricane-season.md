Houston knows storms. So it makes sense to want a strong place to keep your things. A steel box in the yard can hold tools, [patio furniture](/for/homeowners/), cleanup gear, or [extra stock for a shop](/for/businesses/).

The best time to set one up is not in a June rush. It is in the calm months, when you can take your time. This guide shows you how to buy a used container in Greater Houston, where to set it, and what to check first.

## Hurricane season has an end date

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from **June 1 to November 30**. That comes from the National Hurricane Center.

So as fall goes on, the calm months get close. That is a good time to plan. You can pick your spot. You can get the ground ready. You can check the rules. None of that has to happen with a storm on the way.

Here is the simple plan:

1. **Now:** pick a spot and check your local rules.
2. **Next:** get the ground firm and level.
3. **Then:** order, and get your delivery window.
4. **By spring:** load it up and get organized before the next season starts.

## A storage box, not a storm shelter

This part matters most, so we will say it plainly.

**A shipping container is storage. It is not a storm shelter.** Do not stay inside one during a hurricane or a bad storm.

A real safe room is a different thing. FEMA says a safe room is built to meet FEMA's own design criteria, set out in a guide called FEMA P-361. That guide also draws on a storm shelter standard called ICC 500. A used storage container is not built or sold for that job.

When a storm comes, follow what your city or county tells you to do. That includes evacuation orders. Your container's job is to keep your stuff safe and dry, not you.

Thinking about tying the box down? That is a fair question on the Gulf Coast. Talk to a licensed local contractor or engineer. They can look at your spot and tell you what makes sense. Your city or county may have rules on it too.

## What we stock

We keep this simple. We stock one grade: **used Wind and Water Tight (WWT)**.

WWT means the box keeps out rain, wind, snow, and pests. The doors seal. There are no holes. It is not shiny. You may see surface rust, dents, or scuffs from its years at sea. That is normal. Our [condition guide](/condition/) shows what to expect.

Some people search for new containers in Houston. Here is the honest answer:

- **New (one-trip) and Cargo Worthy** containers are by special request only. Ask us, and we will tell you what we can get for your ZIP.
- **A 10ft container** is also a special request.
- **We do not sell or install heaters.**

For most storage jobs, a used WWT box does the work just fine.

## Pick a spot that stays firm and dry

Much of the Houston area is flat. Many Gulf Coast soils are heavy clay that drains slowly. Much of the metro sits in or near mapped floodplains. The Harris County Flood Control District says a major flood happens somewhere in the county about every two years.

So where you set the box matters a lot. Water should run away from it, not sit under it.

Look for a spot that is:

- **Firm.** The ground should not get soft and muddy after rain.
- **Level.** A level box lets the doors open and close the right way.
- **A little higher.** Pick ground that sits above the low spots in your yard.
- **Easy to reach.** The delivery truck needs a clear path in and room to set the box down. Our [delivery page](/delivery/) explains what to plan for.
- **Clear in front.** Leave room for the doors to swing all the way open.

A gravel pad or solid blocks under the corners can help. They lift the box off the wet ground and help water drain away.

Getting the ground ready is your part. Ours is planning the drop with you. We plan placement on firm, raised ground. We can also scout a tight or soft site before we schedule. If you are not sure about your spot, just ask.

## Gulf air and wet walls

Houston air is often warm and humid. That can lead to water drops on the inside of a closed steel box. It can look like a leak. Most of the time it is not. It is condensation.

The fixes are simple. Vents, drying packs, and keeping things off the floor all help. We explain it all in [why a container "rains" inside](/blog/why-does-my-container-rain-inside-condensation/).

## Houston rules work a little differently

Houston handles land use in its own way, so ask the City of Houston Planning and Development Department how its rules apply to your lot.

The city still has development rules. Your neighborhood may have deed restrictions or HOA rules. Floodplain rules may also apply, from the city or from your county.

**We do not decide whether you need a permit.** Rules are set by your city, your county, and your neighborhood. Checking them is the buyer's job. Please do it before you buy.
