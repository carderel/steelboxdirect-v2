The holidays bring more stock than a back room was built to hold. Boxes stack up in the hallway. The office turns into a stockroom. You trip over cartons on the way to the register.

A steel storage container behind your shop can take that load. But there is a catch, and it is about time. **Almost all deliveries take about two weeks.** So if you want a box in place before the rush, you have to decide early.

This guide gives you the plan. It covers when to order, which size to pick, where to put it, and how to keep your stock safe inside.

## The clock starts now

Here are the dates that matter for 2026:

- **Thanksgiving:** Thursday, November 26
- **Black Friday:** Friday, November 27

Almost all deliveries take about two weeks. We'll give you an honest window before you commit. But we can't promise a holiday date, and we won't pretend to.

So let's do the math out loud. If you order in early November, about two weeks puts your box in the middle of the month. That leaves time to load it before Thanksgiving week. If you wait until mid-November, the two weeks can run right into the holiday itself. That is cutting it too close.

**The plain answer: if you want holiday storage, order early.** Late October is a good time to get a quote. Early November is about as late as we'd want to start.

## A simple week-by-week plan

Work back from the day you need the space.

1. **This week:** Measure the spot. Check your lease and talk to your landlord. Check city rules.
2. **Next:** [Get a quote](/quote/) with your ZIP and the size you want. Ask for your delivery window.
3. **Then:** Order. Clear the path for the truck and get the ground ready.
4. **Delivery day:** The box comes off the truck and sits where you planned.
5. **Before the rush:** Load it, label your shelves or stacks, and lock it up.

If any step takes longer than you think, the early start is what saves you.

## Which size fits a shop?

The two main choices are a 20ft and a 40ft. The width and height are the same. Only the length changes.

- **20ft:** about 148 square feet of floor inside. It fits a small spot behind a building, a corner of a lot, or a tight loading zone.
- **40ft:** about 302 square feet of floor inside. That is about twice the room, and it is usually the better value per square foot when you have space for it.
- **40ft High Cube:** same footprint as a 40ft, but one foot taller inside. Pick it if you stack high or plan to add your own shelves.

Two things to know before you choose:

- **20ft containers are getting harder to find.** Our supplier told us so in October 2026. They are still out there, but you may see fewer choices. That is one more reason to order early. Our [20ft vs 40ft guide](/blog/20ft-vs-40ft-shipping-container/) explains why.
- **A 10ft container is a special request.** If you only have room for a tiny box, ask, and we'll tell you what we can get.

Here is a good rule. Storage fills up whatever space you give it. If the spot fits a 40ft, think hard before you go smaller.

## Check your lot before the truck comes

A container is driven in, not carried in. On a tilt-bed truck, the box slides off the back as the truck pulls forward. So the truck needs room.

Walk your lot and look for these:

- **A straight path in.** The truck needs a long, straight run to the spot.
- **Enough width.** Watch for gates, poles, bollards and parked cars.
- **Enough height.** Look up. Branches, wires, awnings and signs can block the way.
- **Firm ground.** The ground on the route and at the spot has to hold a heavy truck.
- **Room for the doors.** The doors are on one end. Leave space for them to swing all the way open, and face them where you'll load.

Our [delivery page](/delivery/) lists the exact clearance numbers. If your lot is tight, tell us. We plan placement with you, and we can scout a tight or soft site before we schedule. When a truck can't make the drop, a crane-set delivery may be an option.

## Keep your holiday stock safe and dry

A steel box keeps out rain. It does not control the air inside. That matters a lot for boxed goods.

**Watch out for condensation.** When the air cools at night, water drops can form on the inside walls and ceiling. It can look like a leak. Most of the time it is not. It tends to be worst when warm days are followed by cold nights, which is common in fall and early spring. Our guide on [why a container "rains" inside](/blog/why-does-my-container-rain-inside-condensation/) explains it.

**Cardboard is the weak spot.** Cardboard soaks up moisture like a sponge. Damp boxes get weak and can fall over when stacked. So:

- **Get stock off the floor.** Pallets keep boxes out of any water on the floor.
- **Leave a gap at the walls.** Don't press boxes flat against the cold steel.
- **Use drying packs.** Desiccants are small packs that soak up water from the air.
- **Use plastic totes** for anything you'll keep in there past the holidays.

**Some things should stay in the store.** A closed container can get very hot in summer and very cold in winter. There is no thermostat in a steel box. Food that can spoil, live plants, and electronics that hate heat and damp do not belong inside. See our list of [12 things never to store in a container](/blog/12-things-never-store-in-a-shipping-container/) before you load.

**Lock it up.** Use a strong lock. Before you move stock in, call your insurance agent. Ask what lock they want to see and how they treat stock kept outside the building. Your policy is between you and your carrier.

## Your landlord and your city come first

**We do not decide whether you need a permit or permission.** That is your call to check, and it is the buyer's job.

Before you buy, ask:

- **Your landlord or property owner.** Can a container sit on the lot? Where? For how long?
- **Your lease.** Some leases have rules about outdoor storage or parking spaces.
- **Your business park or shopping center**, if you're in one.
- **Your city or county.** There may be rules on placement, parking, fire lanes and exits.
