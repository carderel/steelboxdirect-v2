Buck season is coming. You have your stands picked out. You know where the deer move. But where does all the gear go between trips?

A lot of hunters toss it in a truck bed, a barn, or a tarp pile. A steel shipping container is a better spot. It locks. It keeps out rain and snow. And it sits right on your land, so the gear is there when you are.

This guide is for hunters in the Huntington area and across western West Virginia. It covers what a camp box is good for, what to keep out of it, and how to get one on your land in time.

## The buck-season clock

In West Virginia, the buck firearms season traditionally opens on the Monday before Thanksgiving. In 2026, that is Monday, November 23. Check the [WV Division of Natural Resources](https://wvdnr.gov/hunting/hunting-regulations/) hunting regulations for the exact dates where you hunt. Logan, McDowell, Mingo and Wyoming counties have no buck firearms season.

## What a camp container is good for

A container is a steel box with a floor and two big doors. It is plain. That is the point. It holds the bulky stuff that is a pain to haul back and forth:

- Ladder stands and hang-on stands
- Ground blinds and blind chairs
- Camp chairs, folding tables and cots
- Hand tools, saws, loppers and rope
- Trail cameras between uses
- Tarps, buckets and plastic totes of camp gear

For most camps, [a 20-foot box is plenty](/shipping-containers-for-sale/20-foot-shipping-container/). It fits on a smaller flat spot and holds plenty. [A 40-foot box gives you more room](/shipping-containers-for-sale/40-foot-shipping-container/) if your group is big. Not sure? Our [20ft vs 40ft guide](/blog/20ft-vs-40ft-shipping-container/) walks through it. A 10-foot container is a special request. Ask us and we will see what we can do.

Everything we stock is used and Wind and Water Tight (WWT). That means it keeps out rain, wind and snow. It may have dents and rust spots. It will not look new. Our [condition guide](/condition/) shows what WWT covers.

## What to leave out of the box

A container keeps weather out. It does not control heat, cold or smells inside. A few things do not belong in a camp box.

### Food and feed

West Virginia is black bear country. A steel box is not made to seal in smells. Food can also bring in mice, and mice chew through bags, boxes and cords. Keep food in a sealed, bear-safe spot. Take it home when you leave camp.

### Fuel and anything that burns

Do not store gas cans or propane tanks in a closed container. Propane tanks belong outdoors in a vented spot. Not sure where to keep gas cans? Ask your local fire department. And never run a generator, engine or fuel-burning heater inside the box. Burning fuel makes carbon monoxide. You cannot see it or smell it. Read [why a container is not airtight](/blog/are-shipping-containers-airtight/) before you plan any heat.

We do not sell or install heaters.

### Guns and ammo

A shipping container is not built as secure storage for guns or ammo. Rules for storing them vary. Follow state and federal rules, and take them home with you. Our list of [12 things you should never store in a container](/blog/12-things-never-store-in-a-shipping-container/) covers this and more.

## Getting a box onto hill and hollow land

This is the hard part in West Virginia. A lot of good hunting ground is steep. Lanes are narrow. Creek bottoms get soft after rain.

The box does not get carried in. It gets driven in on a big truck. Our [delivery page](/delivery/) lists what a site needs. Here are the big ones:

- **Width:** 12 feet of clear room at the narrowest point.
- **Height:** 14 feet overhead, free of branches and wires.
- **Ground:** firm enough for a 30,000 lb truck.
- **Approach:** 100 feet or more of straight path for a tilt-bed truck.

A long lane up a ridge can be fine. A sharp turn, a low limb or a soft spot can stop the truck cold. Walk your route before you order. Look up, not just down. Send us photos of the lane and the spot you want with your quote request. We plan placement and can scout tight or soft sites before we schedule.

Pick a spot that is flat and firm. Higher ground is better than a creek bottom. Gravel or blocks under the box help keep it level and up out of standing water. If the ground is too rough for a truck, there may be other ways to set it, so ask.

## Leaving it alone all season

A camp box sits by itself for weeks at a time. A few habits help.

**Lock it.** Use a strong padlock. A box out of sight from the road is easier to get into, so a good lock matters.

**Plan for condensation.** Cold nights and warmer days can make water drip from the ceiling inside. It is not a leak. Keep gear off the floor and in plastic totes. Our guide on [why a container "rains" inside](/blog/why-does-my-container-rain-inside-condensation/) explains the fixes. If you want vents or other changes, you can hire a local pro after delivery. We do not do that work.

**Check it after storms.** Look for fallen limbs on the roof and water pooling near the base. Make sure the doors still close tight.
